# CASE 002 — CONTENT DESIGN BRIEF

> **Status:** design candidate. Not implemented, not registered, not runtime data.
> Nothing in this document has been added to `src/content/`. This is a proposal
> for review, written to the shape of the existing content types so that an
> approved version can later be authored as data without redesign.

---

## 1. Case title (working)

**«الدور»** — *El-Dor*

English gloss for internal use only: **The Turn**.

The word carries three meanings at once, all of them live in this case:

- **الدور** — your turn in the جمعية (the month the pot is handed to you)
- **الدور** — the floor of a building
- **الدور** — the part a person plays in front of others

Working subtitle (Arabic, player-facing):

> **كل واحد جه دوره.**

Alternate subtitle if a softer read is preferred: **الجمعية بتمشي بالثقة. والثقة مالهاش ورق.**

---

## 2. One-sentence pitch

On the Thursday night the جمعية money is finally handed over, the envelope
disappears from a drawer in a flat nobody left — and the four neighbours in the
room discover that the money was never the thing anyone was actually protecting.

---

## 3. Player experience promise

Case 001 asks: *what is this family refusing to say about the dead?*

Case 002 asks: **what will you admit about your own money in front of people
who see you every single day?**

The promise to the table:

- No one died. The stakes are live, immediate, and next-door.
- Every player is carrying financial shame. That is the engine.
- Every player has a fact that helps the room and costs them something to say.
- Roughly halfway through, the money is found — and the case stops being a theft
  and becomes something more uncomfortable.
- The reveal is not "who is the criminal." It is "who was carrying everyone,
  and what it cost her that you asked to see the numbers."

Target feeling at the end: a table that argues for ten more minutes after the
app has finished — about whether Nabil was wrong to ask.

---

## 4. Setting

**Where:** A six-flat residential building on a side street — an older,
respectable, not-wealthy Cairo neighbourhood. Ground-floor flat belonging to
**سعاد**, who has run the building's جمعية for two years.

**When:** A Thursday evening, present day. Around 9pm.

**What a جمعية is** (must be stated in one line in the case intro, because the
whole case rests on it): six neighbours each put in ١٥٬٠٠٠ every month; every
month one of them takes the whole ٩٠٬٠٠٠. There is no contract, no bank, no
paperwork. It runs on nothing but the fact that these people have to look at
each other in the stairwell tomorrow.

**Why nobody leaves:** The flat door is standing open onto the stairs, as it
always is. The circle is closed socially, not physically.

> محدش يقدر يمشي، مش عشان الباب مقفول — عشان اللي هيمشي هيبقى هو.

That single line is the closed-circle premise and it should appear in the intro.

**Tonight's cast:** Four of the six members are present. **أم نادر** is at her
daughter's in Tanta and sent her share with the doorman; **رمضان** paid his
share on Tuesday. (This is also the expansion slot — see §22.)

**Sensory register for later art/audio direction:** a salon kept for guests,
plastic still on one armchair, a بوفيه with a glass front, tea being made twice,
the stairwell light on a timer that keeps cutting out, the sound of a football
match from the flat upstairs. Warm, not shabby. Nobody here is poor. Several
people here are one bad month from looking poor, which is worse.

---

## 5. Central incident

At **7:10pm**, in front of the room, Souad puts the envelope — ٩٠٬٠٠٠ جنيه,
this month's pot — into the drawer of the buffet in the salon. It is Hoda's
turn. Hoda's daughter's حفلة is in ten days and the hall's balance is due.

At **8:10pm**, Nabil says, out loud, in front of everyone:

> قبل ما تتسلّم — نعدّها قدام الكل.

At **9:00pm**, Souad opens the drawer to hand the envelope over.

It is not there.

Nobody has left the building. Nobody wants to be the first to say a name.

**The question put to the table:** مين شال الظرف؟

## 6. Canonical truth

*(Written first. Everything downstream is derived from this.)*

**Mostafa moved the envelope.** At about 8:15pm, five minutes after Nabil
demanded a public count, he went into the flat "to get his charger," took the
envelope out of the drawer, and pushed it behind the water heater on the kitchen
balcony. He did not take it out of the flat. He never intended to keep it.

**He did it to stop the count.** He has not paid his share for three months,
including this one. If the money is counted in front of everyone it comes up
**١٥٬٠٠٠ short**, and the only question in the room after that is *who didn't
pay*. He had perhaps a minute to think and no plan beyond "not tonight."

**Souad has been covering him.** For the three previous months she quietly put
her own money into the pot — **٤٥٬٠٠٠ in total** — so that whoever's turn it was
got the full amount and nobody had to be told that Mostafa was short. She wrote
"تم" next to his name each month. Ten days ago she told him, gently, that she
could not do it a fourth time. He did not believe her.

**Nabil's arithmetic is correct and his conclusion is wrong.** He is an
accountant. Over three months he added up what he had heard people say they'd
paid and what people said they'd received, and found roughly ٤٥٬٠٠٠ that does
not reconcile. He is right that the number exists. He has decided it means Souad
is skimming. It means the opposite.

**Nabil has been out of work for two months.** He leaves the building at 7:40
every morning carrying his bag and comes back at 5. His wife knows. Nobody else
does. He wants out of the جمعية with his contributions accounted for, and he
cannot say why without saying that.

**Hoda paid a ٤٠٬٠٠٠ deposit on the hall eleven days ago with borrowed money**
at interest, against a pot she had not yet received. She went into the salon
alone at 7:40 and opened the drawer to check the envelope was really there. She
did not touch it. Saying she opened it makes her the obvious suspect, so she
says she was never in the salon alone.

**And underneath all of it:** two years ago Hoda missed a payment. Her brother
told her he had handled it. He never did. **Souad covered it**, said nothing to
anyone, and let the brother keep the credit — because a woman whose brother
takes care of her is a different thing in that stairwell than a woman who
couldn't pay.

**What the case is actually about:** The جمعية never ran on money. It ran on one
woman quietly absorbing everybody's bad month so that nobody in that building
ever had to be *seen* having one. What broke it was not a theft. It was someone
asking to see the numbers.

| 7:40 | Hoda is alone in the salon ~6 minutes. She opens the drawer, sees the envelope, closes it. | Hoda (at a cost) |
| 7:55 | Mostafa jokes about the pot being heavy. Souad does not laugh. | all |
| 8:05 | Souad opens the drawer for her glasses. Envelope is there. | Souad |

| 8:12 | Tea is made again. Two people on the balcony, one in the kitchen. | contested |
| 8:15–8:20 | **Mostafa goes into the flat for his charger. He moves the envelope.** | Mostafa |

**Designed alibi geometry:** every one of the four has an unexplained gap
between 7:25 and 8:25, and every one of them has an innocent reason for it that
is embarrassing to give. That is the whole discussion economy of this case.

## 8. Core characters

Four playable characters for the first version. No scripted dialogue — voice
notes only.

### 8.1 — سعاد عبد الحميد (Souad Abdel-Hamid), 56

**PUBLIC IDENTITY** — Ground floor. Retired school administrator. Runs the
جمعية and has done for two years. In this building, "سعاد ماشية الجمعية" is a
statement about her character, not her admin duties.

**PERSONALITY** — Unhurried. Deflects with hospitality — a question she doesn't
want gets answered with tea. Speaks in proverbs when cornered. Genuinely kind,
and the kindness is also how she keeps her standing.

**RELATIONSHIPS** — Knew Hoda's mother. Treats Mostafa as family and calls him
"يا ابني". Respects Nabil and finds him exhausting. Everyone in the building
owes her something small.

**WHAT THEY KNOW** — The envelope was in the drawer at 8:05. Mostafa has not
paid for three months. She covered ٤٥٬٠٠٠ of it herself. She told him ten days
ago she could not do it again. Two years ago she covered Hoda's missed month.

**WHAT THEY BELIEVE** — That naming Mostafa in that room would finish him in the
building, and that a man finished in his own stairwell doesn't recover.

**WHAT THEY SUSPECT** — She knows almost immediately. Within twenty minutes of
9:00 she is certain it was Mostafa. She says nothing.

**WHAT THEY ARE HIDING** — Everything above. Also: that the reason the pot could
survive three short months is her own savings, which are now gone.

**WHAT THEY WANT** — For the night to end with Hoda paid and nobody publicly
shamed. In that order, and she knows the two are now incompatible.

**WHAT THEY FEAR** — Being seen as someone who lied in a notebook. Her entire
position in that building is that her word does not need paper.

**WHAT THEY CAN REVEAL** — The 8:05 sighting (free). The existence of a second
column of numbers (costly). Who it was for (she will not, unless pushed hard).

**WHAT THEY DO NOT KNOW** — That Mostafa hid it rather than took it. That Nabil
has been keeping his own figures. That Nabil is out of work. That Hoda borrowed
at interest.

---

### 8.2 — هدى (Hoda), 41

**PUBLIC IDENTITY** — Second floor. Runs a small tailoring business out of the
front room. Widowed. Her daughter's حفلة is in ten days. This month's pot is
hers and everybody knows it.

**PERSONALITY** — Fast. Answers a question with a question. Defensive before
she's been accused, which reads as guilt and isn't. Funny when she's not
frightened.

**RELATIONSHIPS** — Close to Souad, in the way of someone who has needed her.
Openly irritated by Nabil, who she thinks has been trying to take her turn.
Fond of Mostafa and treats him as a kid.

**WHAT THEY KNOW** — She was alone in the salon at 7:40. She opened the drawer.
The envelope was there. She paid ٤٠٬٠٠٠ on the hall eleven days ago with money
she borrowed at interest, and the balance is due in ten days.

**WHAT THEY BELIEVE** — That Nabil engineered tonight to embarrass her, because
he asked for her month two months ago and was refused.

**WHAT THEY SUSPECT** — Nabil. Loudly. And with a real reason, which is what
makes her wrong instead of stupid.

**WHAT THEY ARE HIDING** — The borrowing. The 7:40 visit to the drawer. And a
smaller, older thing she has decided not to think about: that two years ago she
missed a month and never confirmed with anyone that it was actually settled.

**WHAT THEY WANT** — The ٩٠٬٠٠٠, tonight, without anyone in that stairwell
finding out how close she is to the edge.

**WHAT THEY FEAR** — Being pitied. Specifically: being a woman the building
takes up a collection for.

**WHAT THEY CAN REVEAL** — That the envelope was in the drawer at 7:40 — which
narrows the window enormously and simultaneously puts her alone in the room
with it.

**WHAT THEY DO NOT KNOW** — That her brother never paid that month. That Souad
did. That Souad has been covering Mostafa. That Nabil isn't working.

---

### 8.3 — مصطفى (Mostafa), 33

**PUBLIC IDENTITY** — Fourth floor. Works in — and tells people he is about to
open a second — mobile phone shop. Souad's late friend's son; treated as the
building's nephew.

**PERSONALITY** — Warm, loud, funny, first to carry something heavy for someone.
Jokes harder the more cornered he gets, which is a tell. "والله العظيم" arrives
in his speech about four sentences before a lie.

**RELATIONSHIPS** — Calls Souad "يا طنط" and means it. Genuinely likes Hoda and
genuinely wants her to get her money. Finds Nabil humiliating to be near.

**WHAT THEY KNOW** — He moved the envelope. Where it is. That he hasn't paid for
three months. That an import deal went bad in the spring and took everything.
That Souad told him ten days ago she couldn't cover him again.

**WHAT THEY BELIEVE** — That Souad covered him *completely* and that the
shortfall is his alone and only ١٥٬٠٠٠. He has no idea she is ٤٥٬٠٠٠ out of
pocket, and no idea she ever did the same for anyone else.

**WHAT THEY SUSPECT** — Nothing useful. He is the only person in the room not
theorising, and that is visible.

**WHAT THEY ARE HIDING** — All of it. Above all: that there is no second shop,
there was never going to be a second shop, and the money for it is gone.

**WHAT THEY WANT** — To get to the end of the night still being the person who
carries things upstairs for people. He would rather be suspected of theft by
three people than known to be broke by six.

**WHAT THEY FEAR** — Souad finding out he let her pay for him and then did this
in her flat.

**WHAT THEY CAN REVEAL** — Where the envelope is. That is the whole game for
him, and every minute he doesn't makes it worse.

**WHAT THEY DO NOT KNOW** — That Souad already knows. That Nabil saw him leave
the flat at 8:20. That Souad only covered part of it and is now out of savings.

---

### 8.4 — نبيل (Nabil), 49

**PUBLIC IDENTITY** — Third floor. Accountant at a firm in Dokki. Precise,
punctual, the man who has the building's water bill history in a folder.

**PERSONALITY** — Slides into semi-formal Arabic when he's angry — "المفروض
يكون في ورق" — which the others find pompous. Not cold; rigid, and rigid because
he is frightened. Has been humourless for exactly two months and nobody has
connected the dates.

**RELATIONSHIPS** — Formal with Souad; he respects her and is about to accuse
her. Friction with Hoda over the turn order. Contempt for Mostafa that he thinks
he hides.

**WHAT THEY KNOW** — He demanded the count at 8:10. He was on the stairwell from
8:15 to 8:25 and saw Mostafa come out of the flat. He has been keeping his own
running figures for three months and there is about ٤٥٬٠٠٠ he cannot reconcile.
He has not had a job since June.

**WHAT THEY BELIEVE** — That Souad has been taking from the pot. He is not
malicious about it; he thinks he is the only one willing to look.

**WHAT THEY SUSPECT** — Souad, primarily. Mostafa, secondarily and vaguely,
because he saw him — but a phone shop kid is not who you'd suspect of a
٩٠٬٠٠٠ envelope, and he knows how it will sound.

**WHAT THEY ARE HIDING** — That he is unemployed. That he asked Souad two months
ago to move his turn forward and she refused. That the reason he was on the
stairs at 8:15 was a call from a recruiter, which is the one thing that would
clear the timeline.

**WHAT THEY WANT** — To be shown to be right, to get his money out, and to leave
this building's finances behind before anyone finds out he has no salary.

**WHAT THEY FEAR** — The word "عاطل" attached to his name on his own staircase.

**WHAT THEY CAN REVEAL** — The 8:20 sighting of Mostafa. It is the single most
valuable fact in the room and he can only produce it by explaining why he was
standing in a stairwell at 8:15 pretending to have signal problems.

**WHAT THEY DO NOT KNOW** — That his ٤٥٬٠٠٠ is Souad's own money going *in*, not
coming out. That Mostafa hasn't paid. That Hoda borrowed.

## 9. Character relationships

```
                       سعاد
              (carries everyone, says nothing)
             /            |             \
      covered a       covered 3         respects,
      month for       months for        about to accuse
      2 yrs ago       (secret)          /
           |              |            /
         هدى  ←── refused turn swap ── نبيل
           |         (real grievance)   |
      thinks brother                    | saw him leave the flat
      paid for her                      ↓
           \                          مصطفى
            \___ treats him as a kid ___/
                 (and he likes her,
                  and that's why the
                  envelope is behind
                  the water heater
                  and not in his pocket)
```

Three relationship facts carry the case:

1. **Souad → Mostafa** is a debt he doesn't know the size of.
2. **Souad → Hoda** is a debt she doesn't know exists.
3. **Nabil → the room** is the only honest demand made all night, and it is the
   thing that breaks everything.

## 10. Motives

| Character | Apparent motive to take ٩٠٬٠٠٠ | Real motive tonight |
|---|---|---|
| سعاد | Cover a shortfall she created | Prevent a public count that will shame a boy she raised half of |
| هدى | ٤٠٬٠٠٠ borrowed at interest, balance due in ten days | Get her turn without her desperation becoming stairwell news |
| مصطفى | Debts from a collapsed import deal | Prevent a public count that will name him |
| نبيل | Unemployed, needs cash, was refused this month's turn | Be proven right and exit cleanly before anyone learns he's out of work |

Note the shape: **two characters share the same real motive and neither knows
it.** Souad and Mostafa both want the count stopped. Only one of them acted.
That symmetry is the case's best trap — the table will find the motive and
attach it to the wrong person at least once.

Also note: **the strongest apparent motives (Hoda, Nabil) belong to the innocent
parties, and the weakest apparent motive belongs to the answer.** Fair, because
the correct motive is not "needed money" — it is "needed the counting to not
happen," and that motive is discoverable from the timeline alone.

## 11. Secrets

Secrets in this case are ranked by what they cost to say, not by how guilty they
sound. This is the pressure system.

| Secret | Holder | Cost to reveal | Value to the room |
|---|---|---|---|
| I opened the drawer at 7:40 | هدى | High — makes her the suspect | High — closes the window to 7:40–9:00 |
| I was on the stairs 8:15–8:25 and saw Mostafa leave the flat | نبيل | High — must explain the recruiter call | **Decisive** |
| Someone in this جمعية hasn't paid for three months | سعاد | Very high — she's protected him for three months | Decisive |
| I borrowed ٤٠٬٠٠٠ at interest | هدى | Very high — pity | Low — explains a receipt, clears nothing |
| I have no job | نبيل | Very high | Medium — explains his behaviour all night |
| I moved it | مصطفى | Total | Ends the case |
| Two years ago Hoda missed a month and I paid it | سعاد | She will not say this at the table | Reserved for the reveal |

**Design rule enforced throughout:** *a secret is never a confession.* Three of
the four players are hiding something that looks exactly like guilt and is not.

## 12. Knowledge graph

`K` = knows it as fact · `B✗` = believes something false about it · `S` =
suspects · `—` = blind to it.

| Fact | سعاد | هدى | مصطفى | نبيل |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Envelope in drawer at 7:10 | K | K | K | K |
| Envelope still there at 7:40 | — | K | — | — |
| Envelope still there at 8:05 | K | — | — | — |
| Count demanded at 8:10 | K | K | K | K |
| Mostafa entered the flat 8:15 | — | — | K | K |
| Mostafa moved the envelope | S | — | K | S (weak) |
| Where the envelope is now | — | — | K | — |
| Mostafa 3 months unpaid | K | — | K | B✗ (thinks all paid) |
| Souad paid ٤٥٬٠٠٠ of her own in | K | — | B✗ (thinks fully covered) | B✗ (thinks skimmed out) |
| The ٤٥٬٠٠٠ gap exists | K | — | — | K |
| Hoda borrowed ٤٠٬٠٠٠ at interest | — | K | — | — |
| Nabil unemployed | — | — | — | K |
| Nabil asked to swap turns | K | S | — | K |
| Souad covered Hoda 2 years ago | K | B✗ (thinks brother paid) | — | — |

**Read the columns, not the rows.** Nobody holds two of the three decisive
facts. The solution exists only in conversation — which is the point of the
product.

**Read the `B✗` row for ٤٥٬٠٠٠:** three characters hold three different beliefs
about the same number, and all three beliefs are sincere. That is the spine of
the middle of the game.

## 13. Evidence plan

Five objects. Each one **reveals** something, **misleads** fairly, **asks** a new
question, and **has a job in the reveal**. Chained by `requires`, exactly like
Case 001's invitation → photograph → letter.

> **Implementation note (not to be actioned now):** `EVIDENCE_TYPES` currently
> contains `invitation | photograph | letter`. This case needs
> `notebook | list | receipt | phoneScreen | envelope`. That is a content-type
> addition — a stylesheet rule and an affordance label per the existing
> architecture — and belongs to implementation, not to this document.

### E01 — دفتر الجمعية (The Notebook) · `notebook` · `requires: []`

*A school exercise book with a plastic cover, the corners gone soft. Two years
of Thursdays.*

**Fragments**
1. **الغلاف من جوه** — six names, twenty-four months, a grid.
2. **الصفحات** — every square marked **تم**, in the same pen, in the same hand.
   Nobody has ever missed a month. Two years, six people, not one gap.
3. **آخر صفحة** — a column of figures in a different pen. No names. No dates
   beside them. They add to **٤٥٬٠٠٠**.

**Reveals** — the جمعية is documented entirely by one person's word, and there
is money in this book that the grid does not explain.
**Misleads (fairly)** — reads as a second set of books. Nabil will say so out
loud within thirty seconds.
**Asks** — لو الكل دافع، الأربعين ألف دي جاية منين؟
**Job in the reveal** — the column is Souad's own money going *in*. The
"perfect" grid is the kindness, not the fraud.
**Connects to** — Souad (author), Nabil (arithmetic), Mostafa (three of those
squares are lies told on his behalf).

---

### E02 — كشف الأدوار (The Turn List) · `list` · `requires: [e01]`

*A single sheet taped inside the cupboard door, curling at the corners. Written
two years ago when they started.*

**Fragments**
1. **الترتيب** — the twenty-four months and whose turn each one is, in Souad's
   hand.
2. **الشهر ده** — one entry crossed out and rewritten. **نبيل** struck through;
   **هدى** written above it. Different pen. No date, no note, no explanation.
3. **تحت خالص** — in the margin, small: **«تم الاتفاق شفاهة.»** ("Agreed
   verbally.")

**Reveals** — Hoda's turn was moved forward, into Nabil's slot, by Souad.
**Misleads (fairly)** — reads as favouritism, and gives Nabil a clean, visible,
sympathetic grievance. Half the table will pivot to "نبيل عمل الدوشة عشان
اتظلم."
**Asks** — مين اللي غيّر الدور، وليه محدش اتكلم؟
**Job in the reveal** — Souad moved it because the حفلة date was set. The one
line of paper anyone bothered to write says the arrangement was verbal, which is
this case's thesis in four words.
**Connects to** — Souad, Hoda, Nabil.

---

### E03 — إيصال حجز القاعة (The Hall Deposit Receipt) · `receipt` · `requires: [e02]`

*Carbon-copy, pink, folded in three. It fell out of a bag on the stairs and
somebody picked it up.*

**Fragments**
1. **الإيصال** — formal commercial Arabic. The hall's name, the date of the
   حفلة, **مقدم: ٤٠٬٠٠٠ جنيه**, paid eleven days ago. Balance due within ten
   days.
2. **الاسم** — the payer's name is Hoda's.
3. **على الظهر، بقلم رصاص** — a phone number, and next to it two words in
   somebody else's handwriting: **«سلفة — ١٠٪»**.

**Reveals** — Hoda had ٤٠٬٠٠٠ eleven days before her turn came.
**Misleads (fairly)** — the first two fragments make her look like she already
had money and lied about it. This is the case's cruellest fair trap: the table
convicts her on fragments 1–2, and fragment 3 is the thing that explains her —
and humiliates her.
**Asks** — هدى جابت الأربعين دول منين وهي مستنية دورها؟
**Job in the reveal** — establishes that Hoda's desperation is real and that it
gave her every reason to *want* the count to happen, not to prevent it. Her
motive points the wrong way, and that is provable.
**Connects to** — Hoda, and by contrast, Mostafa.

### E04 — تليفون مصطفى (The Phone) · `phoneScreen` · `requires: [e03]`

*Face-up on the arm of the chair. The screen is still awake.*

**Fragments**
1. **الرسايل الطالعة** — to a contact saved as **«المورّد»**:
   *"ابعتلي النهارده بالليل"* … *"الساعة ١٠ هيبقى التمام"* … *"متقلقش"*.
   Sent this afternoon. Reads exactly like a handoff scheduled for tonight.
2. **الرسايل الجاية** — the replies, which change everything:
   *"يا مصطفى بقالك تلات شهور بتقول كده"* … *"أنا مش هستنى أكتر من الأسبوع ده."*
   He is not receiving. He is being chased.
3. **مسودة مش متبعتة** — an unsent draft, to **طنط سعاد**, written at 6:52 this
   evening: *"أنا آسف يا طنط. مش هعرف الشهر ده كمان."* Never sent.

**Reveals** — Mostafa is in debt, has been for three months, and knew before he
walked into that flat that he could not pay.
**Misleads (fairly)** — fragment 1 alone builds a complete, wrong theory:
Mostafa is moving stolen money at 10pm. The table should get to say it out loud
before fragment 2 takes it away.
**Asks** — لو هو اللي مدفعش، الدفتر مكتوب فيه «تم» ليه؟
**Job in the reveal** — this is the object that makes the answer reconstructable.
It supplies motive-to-stop-the-count, and the timestamp 6:52 proves the motive
existed *before* the evening began.
**Connects to** — Mostafa, Souad (the draft is addressed to her), and E01
(it explains the perfect grid).

### E05 — الظرف (The Envelope) · `envelope` · `requires: [e04]`

*Found behind the water heater on the kitchen balcony. Dust on one side. Nobody
puts anything behind the water heater.*

**Fragments**
1. **الظرف** — closed, an elastic band around it, unopened. It was never taken
   out of the flat.
2. **العدّ** — ٧٥٬٠٠٠. It is **١٥٬٠٠٠ short** — exactly one member's share.
3. **تحت الأستك** — a small folded paper in Souad's handwriting: four amounts
   and four dates. Three of them are the last three months. **The fourth is from
   two years ago.**

**Reveals** — this was never a theft. The money never left the room. Someone
hid an object to stop a number being said out loud.
**Misleads (fairly)** — it does not name anyone. It narrows to *whoever feared
the count*, which is still two people as far as the table knows.
**Asks** — ومين تاني كانت سعاد بتغطي عليه؟ ومن سنتين؟
**Job in the reveal** — the fourth date is the hinge of the entire case. It is
placed on the table and *not explained*, ten minutes before the vote.
**Connects to** — all four.

**Chain:** `E01 → E02 → E03 → E04 → E05`.

**Pacing intent:** E05 must not be reachable early. Finding the money reframes
the case from theft to concealment, and that reframe has to land *after* the
table has spent real time accusing each other of theft — otherwise the accusation
phase never happens and the middle of the game collapses.

## 14. Contradiction plan

**MAJOR CONTRADICTION — the drawer timeline.**

- Hoda states early, unprompted, that she was never alone in the salon.
- Souad states that the envelope was in the drawer at 8:05.
- Hoda in fact saw it at 7:40, alone, and cannot say so without becoming the
  suspect.

The window can only be closed to **8:05–9:00** if Souad speaks; it can be closed
to **7:40–9:00** without her. The table will chase this contradiction for a long
time and it resolves into *nothing but shame*. That is deliberate: the case's
first big "gotcha" pays out an innocent explanation, which trains the table to
distrust its own instinct for guilt right before the real signal arrives.

**SECOND CONTRADICTION — the perfect notebook.**

E01 says every member paid every month for two years. The last page says
٤٥٬٠٠٠ came from somewhere. Both are in the same handwriting. Both are true.
There is no innocent reading of that pair *unless* you allow that the author was
paying for someone — and nobody at the table will reach for that first.

**THIRD CONTRADICTION — Nabil's location.**

He says he was in the kitchen. He was on the stairs. He is lying about *where*
in order to hide *why*, and his lie destroys the credibility of the one true
observation that solves the case. When he finally produces the 8:20 sighting, it
arrives from a man who has already been caught lying about his position — so the
table has to decide whether to believe a proven liar, on the strength of a reason
he is humiliated to give.

That is the best single dramatic beat in the case.

**FOURTH, MINOR — Mostafa's charger.**

He said he went in for his charger. His charger is in his pocket. Nobody notices
this for forty minutes, and it is available to any player who thinks to ask
what he actually came back out holding.

## 15. Player theories we want to create

Ranked by how badly we want to hear them said out loud:

1. **«سعاد بتاكل من الجمعية.»** — Nabil's theory. Backed by real arithmetic and
   a real second column. The most respectable wrong answer in the case, and the
   one that hurts most when it collapses.
2. **«هدى خدتها. هي محتاجاها ومستعجلة وكانت لوحدها في الأوضة.»** — Backed by
   opportunity, motive, receipt, and a lie she actually told. Everything about
   it is correct except the conclusion.
3. **«نبيل عمل الدوشة كلها عشان يغطي على نفسه — وهو أصلاً اتظلم في الدور.»** —
   Backed by the crossed-out list and his lie about the kitchen.
4. **«مصطفى بيهرّب فلوس لمورّد الساعة ١٠.»** — Backed by E04 fragment 1. The
   *right man for the wrong reason*, which is the most satisfying thing a
   mystery can hand a player: they were pointing at him for ninety seconds and
   then talked themselves out of it.
5. **«محدش خدها. سعاد نفسها خبّتها عشان متتعدّش.»** — The near-miss theory. It
   is the correct *shape* of the answer with the wrong person in it, and it is
   only one question away from solving the case: *لو هي عارفة إن الفلوس ناقصة —
   ليه هي أصلاً محتاجة تخبّيها وهي اللي ماسكة الدرج؟*

We want the table to reach theory 5 and get it *almost* right. That is where
"I could have figured that out" comes from.

## 16. Reveal concept

Seven layers, running outward from tonight, through the three months, to the two
years. Mapped to the existing `TruthFact` structure.

`immediateAnswerCharacterId: mostafa` · `immediateFactId: who-moved-the-envelope`

| # | Question | Statement | Importance | Evidence | Characters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | مين شال الظرف؟ | مصطفى شاله. | `immediate` | E05 | مصطفى |
| 1 | ليه؟ | مش عشان الفلوس. عشان العدّ. | `core` | E04, E05 | مصطفى |
| 2 | العدّ كان هيبيّن إيه؟ | إن الجمعية ناقصة ١٥ ألف. | `core` | E01, E05 | مصطفى |
| 3 | والتلات شهور اللي فاتوا؟ | سعاد كانت بتدفع عنه من فلوسها. | `core` | E01, E05 | سعاد, مصطفى |
| 4 | يبقى نبيل كان غلطان؟ | حسابه كان صح. استنتاجه كان غلط. وهو نفسه مش شغال بقاله شهرين. | `core` | E01, E02 | نبيل |
| 5 | ومين تاني؟ | هدى. من سنتين. وهي لسه فاكرة إن أخوها هو اللي دفع. | `core` | E05 | سعاد, هدى |
| 6 | طب مين اللي كسر الجمعية؟ | محدش سرق حاجة. الجمعية كانت ماشية على واحدة بتشيل عن الكل في السكات — واللي كسرها إن حد طلب يشوف الأرقام. | `deeper` | E01–E05 | all four |

**Why layer 5 lands.** Hoda spends the entire evening being the most morally
certain person in the room — she is the victim, it is her money, she is owed.
Layer 5 does not make her a hypocrite; it makes her someone who has been carried
and never knew it. Every angry line she said in the discussion re-reads
differently. That is the recontextualization, and it costs the player nothing
except a feeling.

**Why layer 6 is not a moral.** The line does not say Nabil was wrong to ask.
It says what asking cost. The table can — and should — disagree about whether he
should have asked. Case 001 ends on a chain of silence; Case 002 ends on the
price of transparency in a system that was only ever held together by not
looking. Same product, opposite argument.

**Vote handling (mirrors Case 001):** the same seven truths reveal in the same
order regardless of the vote. Only the line describing what the room found
changes. Naming Mostafa is not a verdict, and the reveal must never call him a
thief — he hid an object for a minute's worth of reasons and then couldn't undo
it, which is a thing that happens to people.

## 17. Egyptian Arabic tone guidance

**Register per character.** They must not sound like one writer.

| | Register | Habits | Sample voice (tone reference only — NOT final dialogue) |
|---|---|---|---|
| سعاد | Warm, unhurried, indirect. Proverbs when cornered. | Answers a hard question with hospitality. Says "يا حبيبتي" while refusing. | «اقعدوا اشربوا الشاي الأول.» / «الكلام ده ميتقالش في أوضة فيها ناس.» |
| هدى | Fast, defensive, interrupts. Deflects with a counter-question. | Denies before she's accused. | «إنت بتقول إيه بالظبط؟» / «طب وإنت كنت فين لما—» |
| مصطفى | Loud, joking, over-familiar. Jokes hardest when cornered. | "والله العظيم" is a tell. "يا طنط" is real affection. | «يا جماعة إحنا في فيلم؟» / «والله العظيم يا طنط أنا معنديش فكرة.» |
| نبيل | Precise. Slides into semi-formal Arabic under stress. Speaks in figures. | Says "المفروض" a lot. Uses exact numbers where others round. | «المفروض يكون في ورق.» / «تلات شهور × خمستاشر ألف. اقعدوا احسبوها معايا.» |

**Global rules**

- Natural spoken Egyptian for everything a character says or thinks. Never MSA
  by default.
- **Do not force slang.** One authentic idiom that lands beats four decorative
  ones. If a sentence works plainly, leave it plain.
- Written evidence takes the register its object would really have:
  - **E01 دفتر** — clipped shorthand, single words: «تم» · «الشهر ده» · a date.
  - **E02 كشف** — handwritten domestic Arabic, one formal marginal phrase
    («تم الاتفاق شفاهة») whose formality is the joke.
  - **E03 إيصال** — genuine commercial Arabic, correct and impersonal. The
    coldness of the form against what it means to Hoda is the effect.
  - **E04 تليفون** — real messaging Arabic: no punctuation, dropped letters,
    voice-note-adjacent phrasing. This is the one place register should feel
    almost too casual.
  - **E05 ورقة** — four amounts, four dates, nothing else. No sentence anywhere
    on it. The absence of explanation is the writing.
- Numbers should be written the way people say them at a table
  («خمستاشر ألف»), and the way they're written on a receipt («١٥٬٠٠٠»),
  depending on the object.
- No character explains Egyptian culture to another character. If a line exists
  to inform a foreign reader, cut it.
- Terms that carry weight and should appear **because the situation produces
  them**, not as decoration: **الجمعية · الدور · العِشرة · الواجب · سمعة ·
  «الناس هتقول إيه» · «ده ابن الناس»**. Target: two or three across the whole
  case, in mouths where they belong.

## 18. Inspiration notes

Structural techniques only. No characters, names, dialogue, scenes, plot
sequences, settings, or twists are drawn from any existing work.

- **Closed circle by social constraint** — the exits are open; leaving is the
  confession. Classical technique, relocated to a stairwell.
- **Asymmetric testimony** — no witness holds a complete timeline; the timeline
  exists only as an assembled object.
- **Costly disclosure** — the most valuable fact is held by the person who is
  most humiliated by explaining how they came to know it.
- **The honest arithmetic, the wrong conclusion** — a numerically correct
  discovery pointed at the wrong cause. Fair because the number is real.
- **The misleading object read in stages** — an object whose first fragment
  builds a complete false theory and whose later fragments dismantle it. (Same
  device Case 001 uses on the letter; used here on a phone screen and a
  receipt.)
- **Concealment mistaken for theft** — the crime is reclassified mid-game when
  the "stolen" property is found in the building.
- **The innocent lie that destroys a credible witness** — a witness lies about
  a trivial detail for a private reason, then has to be believed about something
  that matters.
- **Delayed benefactor reveal** — a character learns at the end that a kindness
  she attributed to one person came from another.
- **Final recontextualization of a moral position** — the most righteous voice
  in the room turns out to have been standing on an unknown debt.
- **The answer with the weakest apparent motive** — motive is deliberately
  detached from money and attached to exposure.

| 8 | Are the red herrings fair? | **Pass** | Every misdirection is a true fact with an unstated cause. Nothing is fabricated to mislead. |

**The reconstruction chain — can a table actually get there?**

1. The envelope was in the drawer at 8:05 (Souad, free to say).
2. It is found behind the water heater, unopened, ١٥٬٠٠٠ short (E05).
3. Therefore this was not a theft. Nobody wanted the money; somebody wanted the
   envelope out of the room.
4. The only thing that changed between 8:05 and 9:00 was Nabil's demand at 8:10.
5. Therefore the mover feared the count.
6. The count would reveal a shortfall of exactly one member's share (E05 f2).
7. The notebook says everyone paid (E01) — so the notebook is protecting
   somebody.
8. The phone says who (E04 f2–f3), and the 6:52 timestamp says he knew before he
   arrived.
9. Nabil saw him leave the flat at 8:20.

Nine steps, every one supported by an object or a disclosable fact, and the
final step is optional confirmation rather than the load-bearing beam. **A table
that never gets Nabil to admit where he was standing can still solve this case.**
That is the fairness test that matters: no single player's willingness to be
embarrassed is a hard dependency.

**Guilt-by-secrecy check.** Three of four players are hiding things and are
innocent. Mostafa's secret is not larger than theirs — Hoda's borrowing and
Nabil's unemployment are arguably more shameful. He is not identifiable by
"who is hiding the most." Passed.

## 20. Risks / weak points

**1. Overlap with Case 001 — a hidden ledger and unexplained money. (Highest
risk.)**
Case 001 has altered financial records; Case 002 has a notebook that doesn't
reconcile. On the surface that is the same furniture twice in two cases.

*Why it still separates:* in Case 001 the falsification was self-protective, it
happened years ago, and an innocent woman publicly carried the blame for it. In
Case 002 nothing is falsified for gain, the "false" entries are acts of charity,
they are still happening tonight, and the person they protect is sitting in the
room. One case is about the cost of silence; this one is about the cost of
transparency.

*If the overlap is judged too close on review,* the cheapest fix is to move
Nabil's discovery off arithmetic entirely — he notices that the *order of turns*
has been quietly adjusted more than once, and builds his (still wrong) theory
from favouritism rather than from a missing number. That removes the second
ledger from the case at the cost of one evidence fragment. **Flagged for the
review decision; not pre-emptively applied.**

**2. Mostafa's action must not read as stupid.**
Hiding ٩٠٬٠٠٠ behind a water heater in the flat you're standing in is only
credible under panic and a short clock. The writing has to earn it: Nabil's
demand at 8:10, the room agreeing at 8:12, the door open, ninety seconds. His
plan is not "keep it" — it is "not tonight," and he expects to produce it in the
morning. If the reveal does not make that legible, players will read him as an
idiot rather than as frightened, and the ending loses its warmth. **This is the
single hardest writing problem in the case.**

**3. The premise depends on knowing what a جمعية is.**
Non-Egyptian or younger players may not. Mitigated by one line in the intro and
by the notebook being the first object on the table. Low risk for the target
audience, worth a playtest note.

**4. Money shame can tip into misery.**
Four people humiliated about money for forty-five minutes is a tone risk. The
counterweights are structural, not decorative: Mostafa is funny, Souad is warm,
Hoda is sharp, and the resolution is that everyone was being kind badly. No one
is a villain and the reveal must not produce one.

**5. E05 pacing is fragile.**
Finding the money is the case's best turn and its biggest risk. Too early and
the accusation phase never happens; too late and the reclassification has no
room to breathe. The `requires` chain gates it correctly, but the table controls
pace, and a fast table could reach it in fifteen minutes. Consider an authored
minimum — a discussion beat that must be spent before E05 unlocks. **This is a
content-side constraint that may need an engine affordance; noting, not
requesting.**

**6. Nabil's unemployment is the thinnest thread.**
It does excellent work (explains his aggression, gives him a costly secret,
humanises the accuser) but it is not load-bearing for the solution, so it can
feel bolted on if underwritten. It needs one early behavioural planting — the
bag he carries down every morning — or it will land as a convenience.

**7. Four characters, six جمعية members.**
Justified in-fiction (one travelling, one paid early) and it creates the 5–6
player expansion cleanly, but it is a seam. If a table asks "why isn't everyone
here," the answer must already be in the intro, not invented at the table.

## 21. Recommendation

# **PASS**

Conditional on two things being resolved before authoring:

1. **A decision on risk 1** (ledger overlap with Case 001) — keep as designed, or
   apply the turn-order variant. This is a product-level call about how much
   distance Case 002 needs from Case 001, and it is cheap to change now and
   expensive to change after the evidence is written.
2. **A writing pass specifically on Mostafa's 8:15** (risk 2). If the panic is
   not credible, the case has a hole where its heart should be.

Everything else — premise, timeline, knowledge graph, evidence chain,
contradictions, reveal — holds up under the twelve tests and reconstructs
cleanly in nine steps.

**Strongest aspect:** the reclassification at E05. The moment the money is found
in the flat, every player's model of the evening inverts at once, and the
question changes from *who wanted ninety thousand pounds* to *who was afraid of
a number being said out loud* — which is a question the table can actually
answer from what it already has.

**Weakest aspect:** Mostafa's decision to hide the envelope. It is the one link
in the chain that is psychological rather than logical, and psychology is the
only kind of link a player can refuse to accept.

## 22. Appendix — expansion to 5 and 6 players

Not for the first version. Recorded so the four-player slice is not written into
a corner.

**Fifth: أم نادر (Om Nader), 60s — third floor.** Currently "in Tanta." Present
in the 5-player version. She is the building's memory: she is the one person who
remembers that Hoda missed a month two years ago, and she believes — genuinely,
loudly, and wrongly — that Hoda's brother paid it. She is not malicious; she is a
woman who repeats things. Her arrival lets layer 5 be *discoverable* at the table
rather than reserved for the reveal, which is a meaningfully different game.

**Sixth: رمضان (Ramadan), 38 — first floor, the building's handyman-adjacent
neighbour.** Currently "paid on Tuesday." Present in the 6-player version. He
was on the stairs twice, notices everything, and says nothing because he is the
lowest-status person in the room and knows exactly what an accusation from him
would cost him. His whole design is *a witness who has to be given permission to
speak*, which is a different pressure from Nabil's *witness who has to be
embarrassed to speak*.

Both additions extend the same design thesis — information exists in the room
and is withheld for social, not criminal, reasons — without changing the
canonical truth by a single fact.
